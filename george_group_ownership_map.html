§2Australian Master Hierarchy

The Australian side of the empire is deliberately split into a handful of master holdings (Harringe, George Industries, QANA) that do not operate but hold everything else. Operating entities sit below them in a second layer. Property and IP are typically held in yet another layer so that operational-entity termination cannot cascade into asset loss. Ian and Christine Frazer appear on virtually every master and operating vehicle.

§8The interACT Software Moat — and its limits

What interACT does

interACT is the compliance/scheme-management platform that sits between each state registry and plate production:

  • number availability & reservation
  • sensitive-word / offensive-content filtering
  • approval workflow with registry staff
  • production scheduling into Plate Impressions
  • cancellations, transfers, destructions
  • audit trail / reporting to registry

Why it protects the scheme-operator contracts

Every active Australian state scheme runs on interACT. Replacing Licensys as scheme operator is not a matter of price — it is a multi-year software migration with substantial registry-staff retraining burden. No rational registry wants that risk unless forced.

Where interACT doesn't protect the group

interACT is a scheme-operator layer moat. It does not protect:

  • Manufacturing quality. A technically superior plate can be sold through the existing Licensys-operated scheme if the manufacturer becomes a concession partner.
  • The VIC concession layer. VicRoads runs the concession pathway separately from scheme operator — a manufacturing-only entrant can engage directly.
  • Dealer / retail channels. Fitment-first propositions live outside the scheme-operator flow.
  • Machine-readability upgrades. Optical-test compliance and covert IR can be offered without touching interACT.
Strategic read: Treat interACT as a fortress — do not attack it. Attack the manufacturing layer under the fortress's skirts, via the VIC concession pathway.

§11Strategic Implications for a Custom-Fit Plate Venture

TL;DR: Don't attack the scheme-operator layer. Attack the manufacturing layer via the VIC concession pathway. Use Ian's 75-year age and the succession-uncertainty window as timing leverage — get entrenched before ownership is resolved.
  1. Scheme operator = untouchable in 1 product generation. interACT moat + 25yr incumbency + global standards reference weight via Utsch = unwinnable tender fight.
  2. Manufacturing = vulnerable. Plate Impressions is a Bundaberg stamping factory with mid-teens-to-twenty-percent margins and a family-custodial CEO whose primary energy is elsewhere. Custom-fit + integrated bracket + covert IR machine-readability is exactly the vector a stamping-era factory does not have organisational DNA for.
  3. VIC concession pathway = the foot in the door. VicRoads runs concession approval separately from scheme operator. A technically-differentiated product can enter via Approval 202501_07 or its successor, without ever fighting Licensys for the scheme-operator seat.
  4. Timing window = 2026–2029. 75 is not tomorrow but is within ~3 years of a likely succession inflection. Reach dealer-channel scale and retail visibility before new ownership is resolved = entrenched presence by the time they have to decide whether to fight or absorb.
  5. Counterparty = Christine Frazer, not Ian directly. If the venture ever needs to approach the group (for supply, licence, or acquisition conversation), she is the operational decision-maker. Approach her professionally, not Ian socially.
  6. Watch for Utsch GmbH register changes. Any Utsch shareholder movement will cascade through Avenir/Wilhelmina → Australian beneficial control → the group's strategic posture. This is a tell for when the whole structure may come into play.
